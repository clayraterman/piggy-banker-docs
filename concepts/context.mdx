Context is the background knowledge layer of every client portal — a curated set of durable facts about the business that Piggy and your agents consult every time they answer a question or run a workflow. When you add Context, Piggy stops giving generic financial answers and starts giving answers grounded in how *this specific client's business* actually operates: their revenue model, their fiscal calendar, their key metrics, and the definitions that matter to their team.

## What Belongs in Context?

Context is designed for stable, foundational facts that stay true across many conversations and agent runs. Unlike a chat message that exists only in one conversation thread, Context persists indefinitely and is available to every interaction inside the client portal.

Good candidates for Context entries include:

- **Business model** — how the company makes money (e.g., B2B SaaS with monthly subscriptions, professional services billed hourly, e-commerce with recurring revenue)
- **Key revenue drivers** — the primary variables that move the top line (e.g., number of active subscribers, average contract value, units shipped)
- **Team structure** — departments, headcount breakdown, who owns each budget
- **Fiscal year** — when the company's fiscal year starts and ends; whether it follows a calendar year or a custom fiscal calendar
- **Industry** — the sector the business operates in, relevant benchmarks, and competitive context
- **Important definitions** — how the client defines their most important metrics (e.g., what counts as "churned," how they calculate ARR, what their burn rate formula includes)

<Tip>
When in doubt, add it. Context entries that feel obvious to you — like the fact that this client runs a calendar fiscal year — are exactly the kind of background knowledge Piggy needs to avoid giving answers calibrated to a different type of business.
</Tip>

## Why Context Matters

The difference between a generic AI answer and a genuinely useful financial insight comes down to context. Consider what happens when you ask Piggy, *"What's our runway looking like?"*

**Without Context:** Piggy calculates a generic cash-divided-by-burn figure using whatever definition of burn it assumes by default. The answer may not match how this client actually tracks burn — and it can't account for known upcoming cash events.

**With Context:** Piggy knows that this client defines burn rate as cash out minus cash in (excluding investment proceeds), that they're a pre-revenue SaaS startup, that their fiscal year is calendar-based, and that they track runway in whole months. The answer uses the right formula, speaks in the right terms, and reflects the client's actual financial reality.

Your agents benefit equally. An agent running a weekly cash summary uses the business model and metric definitions in Context to frame its output correctly — without you needing to re-explain the client's situation every time you configure a new workflow.

## Adding and Updating Context

You can add, edit, or remove Context entries at any time. There is no limit to the number of entries you can maintain per client portal.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Context section">
    Navigate to **Context** in the client portal sidebar. You'll see a list of all existing Context entries for this client.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a new entry">
    Click **+ Add Context**. Type the fact you want to record — you can write it in plain sentences, as a label-and-value pair, or as a short definition. Save the entry.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Let Piggy suggest Context">
    Ask Piggy in the chat panel: *"Based on our connected data, what Context entries would you suggest adding?"* Piggy can infer likely business facts from your GL structure, chart of accounts, and connected sources and propose entries for you to approve.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review and update regularly">
    Context is only valuable when it's accurate. After any major business change — a pivot, a new funding round, a restructuring — review the Context section and update any entries that no longer reflect reality.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Example Context Entries

Here is what a well-populated Context section might look like for a B2B SaaS client:

```text
Business model: B2B SaaS, monthly and annual subscription plans
Fiscal year: Calendar year (January 1 – December 31)
Key metric: ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue)
Burn rate definition: Total cash out minus total cash in per calendar month, excluding equity investment proceeds
Revenue recognition: Subscription revenue recognized ratably over the contract period
Primary cost driver: Headcount — payroll and benefits represent approximately 70% of operating expenses
Churn definition: A customer is considered churned when they cancel and their contract term ends, not at cancellation date
Industry: B2B SaaS — HR tech vertical
Funding stage: Series A, raised in March 2024
```

<Tip>
Review your Context entries after every major business change — a pricing model shift, a new product launch, a change in fiscal year, or a significant hiring event. Outdated Context is worse than no Context: it causes Piggy and your agents to confidently give answers based on facts that are no longer true.
</Tip>
