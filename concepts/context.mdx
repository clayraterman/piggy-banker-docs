# Add durable business Context

Context explains **how the business works**. It helps people, Lil Piggy, and agents interpret finance data, but it is not the accounting source of truth.

Context has exactly three sections.

## Company

Record durable identity and operating facts, for example:

- entities, locations, and business model,
- products, services, and customer types,
- ownership and key operating relationships,
- fiscal calendar and reporting conventions, and
- material systems or processes.

## Money

Record policies and interpretations around money, for example:

- revenue and billing model,
- cash policy and restricted cash,
- debt and capital structure,
- material payment terms,
- margin definitions, and
- how management thinks about runway or liquidity.

Connected balances, journal lines, invoices, and statements belong to source data—not Context.

## Strategy

Record the decisions and constraints that should shape analysis:

- goals and current priorities,
- growth or profitability posture,
- major risks,
- thresholds that merit attention,
- planned changes, and
- explicit tradeoffs.

## What belongs where

| Information | Correct home |
| --- | --- |
| “The company recognizes implementation revenue at go-live” | Context |
| March revenue from the ledger | Source data / metric value |
| Target gross margin of 62% | Reusable input or forecast assumption, with supporting Context |
| A Page's comparison period | Page block presentation |
| An agent's Slack channel | Workspace default or deployment override |

## Keep Context trustworthy

- Prefer a small set of clear facts over an unstructured dump.
- State the owner and effective period when a fact can change.
- Review Context after financing events, pricing changes, reorganizations, or strategy shifts.
- Save only deliberate shared notes. Lil Piggy sessions remain private until material is explicitly promoted to client notes.
