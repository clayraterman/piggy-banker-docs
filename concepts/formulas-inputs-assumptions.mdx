# Formulas, inputs, and assumptions

Living models stay understandable when calculated logic, operator inputs, planning assumptions, and business facts have separate roles.

## Choose the right primitive

| Primitive | Use it for | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **System reference** | Connected or plan-derived read-only finance value | Revenue |
| **Saved metric** | Reusable calculated or recorded measure | Gross margin |
| **Reusable input** | Client-wide scalar used by several formulas | Target gross margin |
| **Forecast assumption** | Scenario- or model-specific planning driver | Monthly hiring plan |
| **Context fact** | Durable explanation of how the business works | Billing occurs annually in advance |
| **Page setting** | Local presentation choice | Show trailing 12 months |

## Formula behavior

Formulas are bounded expressions over approved, client-scoped references. The supported vocabulary includes arithmetic and comparisons plus functions such as:

- `IF`
- `SUM`
- `AVG`
- `MIN`
- `MAX`
- `CLAMP`

This is not arbitrary JavaScript or an unrestricted spreadsheet runtime.

A gross-margin metric might conceptually divide Gross profit by Revenue and handle the unavailable or zero-denominator case explicitly. The formula builder resolves references to saved objects rather than trusting free-form names.

## Validation

Formula drafts are evaluated on the server before persistence. Validation rejects or surfaces:

- unknown identifiers,
- references owned by another client,
- unavailable source values,
- ambiguous references,
- direct self-references,
- invalid operations, and
- dependency problems.

Missing values remain unavailable. They are not silently changed to zero to make a formula pass.

## Inputs need governance

For every reusable input or assumption, record:

- a clear name and unit,
- its business meaning,
- an owner,
- an effective period,
- its source or rationale, and
- when it should be reviewed.

## Avoid circular meaning

Even when a dependency graph is technically valid, definitions can still become conceptually circular. Prefer small formulas with named intermediate metrics. A Page should explain the decision; it should not hide an entire finance model inside one opaque expression.
