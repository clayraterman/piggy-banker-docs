title: "Get Started with Piggy Banker: A Five-Step Setup Guide"
sidebarTitle: "Quickstart"
description: "Create your account, add a client portal, supply business context, connect your General Ledger, and build your first financial metric."
---

This guide walks you through everything you need to go from a blank account to a live client portal with real financial data flowing in. By the end, you'll have a connected General Ledger, a working metric, and a solid foundation for building dashboards and automations.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create your account">
    Sign up at [app.piggybanker.ai](https://app.piggybanker.ai) and follow the onboarding prompts.

    When asked to choose a plan, select **CFO Team** if you manage multiple clients, or **Solo** if you're working with a single entity. You can upgrade at any time — pricing is a flat fee per client with optional usage add-ons.

    <Tip>
      During sign-up, Piggy will ask a few questions about your practice to personalize your workspace. Take a moment to answer them — Piggy uses this context across the app.
    </Tip>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add a client portal">
    From your CFO Team dashboard, click **Add Client** and fill in two fields:

    - **Client name** — the name you'll use to identify this client across your dashboard.
    - **Business description** — one or two sentences about what the business does, its industry, and its size.

    Piggy creates a dedicated portal for this client. Every piece of data, every page, and every agent you build lives inside that portal — completely isolated from your other clients.

    <Note>
      You can add as many client portals as your plan allows. Each one is billed at the flat per-client rate.
    </Note>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add business context">
    Open the client portal and navigate to **Context**. This is where you store durable facts about the business — the kind of information Piggy needs to give accurate, relevant answers without you having to re-explain things every session.

    Add facts such as:

    - Fiscal year start and close
    - Primary revenue model (e.g. SaaS subscriptions, project-based services, product sales)
    - Key business drivers and KPIs the client tracks
    - Any known seasonality or one-time events

    <Tip>
      Think of Context as a briefing document for Piggy. The richer it is, the more precise and useful Piggy's responses become across every section of the portal.
    </Tip>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Connect your General Ledger">
    Navigate to **Connections** inside the client portal and click **Add Connection**. Piggy Banker supports three General Ledger providers:

    - **QuickBooks Online**
    - **Xero**
    - **Puzzle**

    Select your provider, complete the sign-in and authorization flow in your browser, then return to the portal. Piggy starts importing your chart of accounts, transactions, and historical periods in the background.

    <Warning>
      A General Ledger connection is required before you can create metrics or build financial dashboards. Piggy cannot generate Pages from GL data until the initial sync completes.
    </Warning>

    Once the sync finishes, Piggy confirms which accounting periods are available. You're ready to build.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Create your first metric">
    Navigate to **Pages** and click **New Page**, then add a **Metric Block**. Give your metric a name — for example, **Monthly Revenue** — and configure its source:

    1. Set **Source** to your connected General Ledger.
    2. Map the metric to the relevant account category (e.g. Income → Total Revenue).
    3. Set the **Date grain** to Monthly and choose your desired lookback range.
    4. Click **Save**.

    Piggy renders the metric immediately. You can ask Piggy to explain any figure, adjust the date range, or build a chart on top of the same data — all without leaving the page.

    <Tip>
      Monthly Revenue is a great first metric, but you can also try **Gross Profit**, **Operating Expenses**, or **Net Cash Position** to quickly validate that your GL data imported correctly.
    </Tip>
  </Step>
</Steps>
