# Design a budget vs. actuals view

A budget-versus-actuals model compares a governed plan with connected accounting results at a compatible grain.

## Define the comparison

Before calculating variance, align:

- entity,
- currency,
- accounting period,
- chart-of-accounts mapping,
- department or other dimension,
- sign convention, and
- version or scenario of the plan.

An annual budget total and a monthly ledger series are not directly comparable until they share a period grain.

## Core metrics

Common measures include:

- Actual,
- Budget,
- Dollar variance,
- Percentage variance,
- Year-to-date actual,
- Year-to-date budget,
- Forecast,
- Forecast versus budget, and
- Full-year outlook.

Define favorable and unfavorable treatment in the Page narrative. A positive expense variance can mean overspend even though the arithmetic result is positive.

## Use dimensions carefully

Department, class, location, project, or entity can support drilldown only when both actual and plan values carry compatible dimension meaning. An unallocated budget should remain visibly unallocated rather than being spread silently.

## Suggested Page structure

1. executive variance summary,
2. revenue and gross-margin drivers,
3. operating-expense table,
4. department or category detail,
5. forecast-to-budget outlook,
6. assumptions and mapping caveats, and
7. owner actions.

## Quality checks

- Reconcile actuals to the selected statement period.
- Confirm the plan version and approval date.
- Test formulas when budget is zero or unavailable.
- Separate mapping gaps from true operating variances.
- Flag stale actuals before generating narrative.
- Preserve plan and actual provenance in downstream agent output.

<Note>
  Plan-entry and Page controls are evolving. This guide defines the finance contract; it does not imply a spreadsheet import or a specific chart interaction that is not present in your workspace.
</Note>
