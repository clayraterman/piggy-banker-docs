Budget vs. actuals is the core of financial performance monitoring — and in most teams it's still a painful monthly ritual of copying numbers between spreadsheets. Piggy Banker replaces that ritual with a live dashboard that updates automatically as your GL closes each period. Define your budget once, connect your actuals, and every variance calculation stays current without any manual intervention.

## What You Need

- **Connected GL with actuals data.** Your general ledger is the source of truth for actual results. See [Connect Your General Ledger](/setup/connect-general-ledger).
- **A budget.** This can be entered manually as a metric, imported from a spreadsheet, or built directly in Piggy Banker. Details below.

## Setting Up Budget Data

You have two options for getting your budget into Piggy Banker:

**Option 1 — Enter budget values manually**

Create a metric for each budget line (e.g., `Budget Revenue`, `Budget Operating Expenses`) and choose **Manual** as the data source. Enter monthly values directly in the metric editor. This works well if your budget is stable and you update it once per quarter.

**Option 2 — Import from a spreadsheet**

If your budget lives in a CSV or Excel file, upload it via **Connections** → **Import CSV**. Map each column to a metric or dimension, and Piggy Banker will create the corresponding metrics automatically. After the initial import you can update the values by re-uploading a revised file.

## Creating Budget vs. Actuals Metrics

Once both your actuals and budget values exist as metrics, create **Variance metrics** that calculate the difference automatically:

- **Revenue Variance** = `Actual Revenue − Budget Revenue`
- **Revenue Variance %** = `Revenue Variance ÷ Budget Revenue × 100`
- **OpEx Variance** = `Actual Operating Expenses − Budget Operating Expenses`
- **Gross Margin Variance** = `Actual Gross Margin − Budget Gross Margin`

Create as many variance metrics as your reporting requires. A positive Revenue Variance means you beat budget; a positive OpEx Variance means you overspent.

## Building the Budget vs. Actuals Dashboard

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a new page">
    Go to **Pages** → **New Page** and name it `Budget vs. Actuals` (or `BvA — [Month Year]` if you want period-specific views).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add a table block">
    Click **+** → **Table** and configure it with the following columns:

    | Metric | Budget | Actual | Variance ($) | Variance (%) |
    |---|---|---|---|---|

    Add one row per metric you want to track (Revenue, Gross Margin, OpEx, Net Income, etc.). Format any row with a negative-favorable variance (e.g., OpEx) with sign inversion so over-budget shows as red. Use **bold** formatting on subtotal rows to make the hierarchy readable at a glance.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add a bar chart comparing Budget vs. Actual by month">
    Click **+** → **Chart** and select both `Budget Revenue` and `Actual Revenue` (or your key metric). Choose **Grouped Bar** as the chart type and set the time window to the current fiscal year. This gives a visual month-by-month comparison that makes it immediately obvious which periods you beat or missed budget.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add a text block for narrative commentary">
    Click **+** → **Text** and write a short summary of the current period's performance: what drove the biggest variances, which are one-time vs. recurring, and what the outlook is for the rest of the year. Update this block each month after close. Keeping the narrative alongside the numbers on the same page makes your BvA deck self-contained.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Automating BvA Reports

Once your BvA dashboard is live, set up an agent to send a formatted summary to Slack at the end of each month — so your team gets the results without having to remember to check the dashboard.

<Tip>
