Cash flow is the most critical metric for almost every business — and it's also one of the most time-consuming to track manually. Piggy Banker eliminates that work entirely. Connect your general ledger and bank accounts once, define your core cash metrics, and you'll have a live, auto-updating cash flow model that always reflects your current position. This guide walks you through every step, from creating your first cash metric to setting up an automated runway alert.

## What You Need First

**Required:**
- A connected general ledger (GL). Your GL is the primary source for inflows, outflows, and operating expense data. See [Connect Your General Ledger](/setup/connect-general-ledger) if this isn't set up yet.

**Optional but recommended:**
- Connected bank account(s). Bank connections provide real-time cash balance data for your Cash on Hand metric, which is more accurate than deriving it from GL entries alone. See [Connect Bank Accounts](/setup/connect-bank-accounts).

## Step 1: Create Your Core Cash Metrics

Build these five metrics to form the foundation of your cash flow model. You can create each one manually, or ask Piggy to create them all at once (see the tip at the bottom of this guide).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Cash Inflows">
    Create a metric named **Cash Inflows** sourced from your GL's receipts and collections accounts — typically accounts receivable payments, direct sales receipts, and any other cash-generating line items. Set the aggregation to **Sum** and the period to **Monthly**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Cash Outflows">
    Create a metric named **Cash Outflows** sourced from your GL's operating expense, payroll, and vendor payment accounts. Include everything that represents cash leaving the business in a given month. Set the aggregation to **Sum** and the period to **Monthly**.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Net Cash Flow">
    Create a metric named **Net Cash Flow** as a formula metric: `Cash Inflows − Cash Outflows`. This gives you a signed monthly figure — positive means the business generated cash, negative means it burned it.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Cash on Hand">
    Create a metric named **Cash on Hand** sourced from your connected bank account balance (if available) or from your GL cash account. This is your point-in-time cash balance — the actual dollars in the bank at any given moment.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Cash Runway">
    Create a metric named **Cash Runway** as a formula metric: `Cash on Hand ÷ Monthly Burn Rate`, where **Monthly Burn Rate** is the absolute value of your average monthly Net Cash Flow over a trailing period (typically 3 months). The result is expressed in months. A runway of 12 means you have 12 months of operating cash at your current burn rate.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Step 2: Build the Cash Flow Dashboard

With your metrics created, put them together on a page so your whole team can see the current position at a glance.

1. Go to **Pages** → **New Page** and name it `Cash Flow Dashboard` (or `Cash & Runway`).
2. Add a **Metric Widget** for each of the five metrics above. Place Cash on Hand and Cash Runway at the top — these are the two numbers your team will check most often.
3. Add a **Chart** block below the metric widgets. Select Cash on Hand as the metric and choose **Line** as the chart type. Set the time window to the trailing 12 months. This gives you a visual trend of your cash balance over time — a rising line signals healthy inflows, a falling line signals accelerating burn.
4. Add a second **Chart** showing Net Cash Flow as a **Bar** chart by month. Positive bars are green (net inflow months), negative bars are red (net burn months).
5. Add a **Text** block at the top of the page with a brief summary or any standing notes (e.g., "Runway calculated on 3-month trailing burn. Bank data syncs daily.").

## Step 3: Set Up a Cash Alert

A cash flow model is only as useful as the alerts it generates. Create an agent that monitors Cash Runway daily and notifies your team the moment it drops below a threshold — so you have time to act before a cash crisis becomes an emergency.

Go to **Agents** → **New Agent** and name it something like `Cash Runway Alert`. Write a prompt like the one below, then set the schedule to **Daily** and the delivery target to your `#finance-alerts` Slack channel.

**Example alert prompt:**

```text
Check Cash Runway daily.
If Cash Runway falls below 6 months, send an alert to Slack #finance-alerts immediately.
Include: current Cash on Hand, Monthly Burn Rate, and projected runway date.
```

Activate the agent. It will check your live Cash Runway metric every day and stay silent unless the threshold is breached — then it fires an immediate Slack alert with the key numbers your team needs to act.

</Tip>
